5. Deep Dive: Why Cap Enterprise Monorepos at 64K Instead of 128K?

Intuitively, larger budgets seem safer. In AST call-graph slicing, however, blindly pushing budgets to 128K degrades both accuracy and speed:

① Diminishing Signal-to-Noise Ratio

Relevant entry points, upstream callers, downstream helpers, and interfaces for a specific task are already 100% captured within 32K–64K. Forcing 128K forces the slicer to pad the remaining 64K with low-relevance peripheral files—pure noise.

② Lost-in-the-Middle Attention Dilution

Even on 128K+ context models, empirical studies show attention weights on mid-prompt details drop sharply past 64K. Extra noise does not improve patch pass rates; it increases edge-case oversights while doubling latency and API costs.

③ Iterative 32K–64K Slices Beat One 128K Dump

For changes spanning 10 microservices in a million-line monorepo, splitting the work into 2–3 focused sub-tasks sliced at 32K–64K each yields far higher first-pass compile accuracy than stuffing everything into one 128K prompt.

⚡ When is 96K – 128K actually justified? Only in two edge cases: (1) Repo-wide signature refactoring where a core interface changes and dozens of downstream implementations must be updated simultaneously; (2) Legacy mega-files where single files exceed 3,000–5,000 tightly coupled lines.
